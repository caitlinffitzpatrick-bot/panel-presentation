"My primary motivation is to be a forever student. I want every day to look a little different. I've led through influence for years because I believe a rising tide lifts all boats. Leading this team formally means helping people who are already senior and self-motivated find real satisfaction in their work, while we grow the impact we have on this business."

Why I want to be a leader, in my own words

What matters most to me, with this team and with other leaders, is the spirit of collaboration: sharing best practices, working through internal challenges together, passing along market intel and recent wins, and analyzing breakdowns in process or results with no ego involved.

Collaboration over ego
Best practices, internal challenges, market wins, and breakdowns in process, all shared openly, analyzed without defensiveness, and treated as team property, not individual credit or blame.
Clarity over comfort
When ownership, process, or next steps are undefined, I ask the tough question early rather than let ambiguity sit. That's been my pattern for years. It's how I've operated even without a title.
Bias toward action
When a client needs urgency, I don't spend time on blame or issue autopsies. I bring the right people together, align on the objective, and move toward resolution.
Calm under pressure
A consistent theme in feedback I've received: calmness, clarity, and pragmatism in complex, high-pressure client situations. That's the tone I want to set for the team.
What will make me successful
  • High standards, paired with watchful-but-not-overpowering management. I want to see the work, not hover over it.
  • A forever-student mindset. I look for the next thing to learn rather than settling into routine.
  • Cross-departmental credibility: relationships built across five departments that most candidates for this role won't have.
We work so independently as a team that I don't yet have full visibility into where our collective knowledge base has weak spots. One of my first moves will be a deliberate assessment, not to grade anyone, but to see where we should be building depth.
The real challenge

Suddenly managing peers

Whoever steps into this seat will face the same thing: suddenly managing people who were peers yesterday. I've lived this once already, when I became a TL manager, and I carry real lessons from it, not just a theory of how it should go.

Closing it: open communication from day one, learning each person's individual development goals, and staying focused on driving the team toward our KPIs together. I'll encourage everyone to keep their relationship with David, find ways to give each person visibility with leadership, and still give autonomy inside clear standards.
Growth area

Negotiation & value positioning

I've been painted into a corner on deals where our opening offer should have been where we landed.

Closing it: I'm completing Black Swan negotiation coursework in 2026, and folding what I learn directly into the team-wide negotiation training I'm proposing, modeling the gap-closing before asking the team to do the same.
What's working
  • A high-achieving group of Type A personalities who genuinely want to win
  • Deep knowledge and real comfort operating in ambiguity
  • Some of the highest tenure and cross-org exposure in the company
What could be better
  • Opportunity assignment still defaults to old habits, like "Kyle's strongest in Parcel, give him this deal," or Sales dictating who they want
  • Implementation fees get waived by default instead of exploring creative structures
  • Solutions typically sets the first offer in proposals, which puts margin at risk if we're not deliberate about it
How I'd drive it forward
Deep bench, not heavy hitters
Deliberate Opportunity Distribution
Replace informal defaults with a systematic approach, paired with a buddy system or shadowing program so everyone gets reps in unfamiliar categories. Front-loaded time investment now builds a genuinely deep, multi-modal bench instead of one or two heavy hitters carrying each category.
Challenge old thinking
Braver, Still-Competitive Deal Structures
Game out the situations we hit repeatedly, starting with default fee waivers, and build creative, market-competitive revenue structures. Pair it with negotiation training focused on protecting margin on the opening offer, since Solutions usually sets it first.
Collaboration first
A Bigger Stage for What We Know
This team carries some of the highest tenure and operational exposure in the company. I want that insight formally used on internal strategic initiatives. Projects like Beon Infinity need exactly the operational context we already have.
